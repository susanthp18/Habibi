SLIDE 1 - TITLE
=====================================================================

This is BigBound AI. It works in loan collections.

The easiest way to explain it is to say what it is not. It is not a voice bot. There
are plenty of those already.

What we built is everything around the voice. The rules, the checks, the memory. The
whole floor that the voice stands on. The voice itself is the smallest part of it.

Fifteen minutes on the slides. Then the real thing running.

SLIDE 2 - 00:12 AM
=====================================================================

(Slow down here. This is the story. Let it breathe.)

Start with one account, on one night.

It is twelve minutes past midnight. A customer's auto debit fails. The bank tried to
pull eighteen thousand four hundred rupees out of his account and the money was not
there. That is the whole event.

Nobody is awake. No alarm goes off. Nothing happens.

Next morning at nine, this account turns up in a report, along with four thousand
others.

Day three, somebody finally calls. No answer. Unknown number, so he does not pick up.

Day twelve, they speak to him for the first time. But his salary has already come and
gone. That money is spent.

Day thirty one, this is a real problem. It goes to the credit bureau. Somebody has to
physically travel to his house, and that costs around twelve hundred rupees.

(Pause.)

Nobody made a mistake here. The telecaller did her job. The system worked exactly as it
was designed. They just lost to the clock.

SLIDE 3 - DELAY, NOT DIALOGUE
=====================================================================

This is the idea the whole product sits on.

Banks in India already know how to collect money. They have been doing it for thirty
years. They are good at it.

Their problem is the first thirty days. High volume, very messy, a lot of rules. And
most of it is still one person with a headset, a screen, and a daily target.

The voice agent is only the mouth. The money is in everything around the call. Who gets
contacted, how fast, on which channel, what we are allowed to offer, and whether the
customer actually paid in the end.

So the starting point was not the call. It was the delay.


=====================================================================
SLIDE 4 - THE FACTORY NOBODY AUTOMATED
=====================================================================

Some numbers on how bad it gets. These are industry numbers, not ours.

Around seventy percent of calls are never answered. Unknown number, nobody picks up.

Twenty to forty percent of a caller's day is not talking. It is typing notes after the
call is over.

Only two to five percent of calls are ever checked for quality. So if a customer
complains, the chance anybody actually heard that call is very small.

And close to half of all field visits, the person is not home. Eight hundred to fifteen
hundred rupees, gone.

Then the last one. The compliance team has to make sure nobody is called after seven in
the evening, nobody on the do not disturb list is called, and nobody is called too many
times. They track all of that in a spreadsheet. By hand.


=====================================================================
SLIDE 5 - SIX JOBS ON ONE FLOOR
=====================================================================

There are six jobs on a collections floor, and we were strict about which ones a
machine should take.

The telecaller, at volume, yes. A hundred and fifty calls a day of the same
conversation is a machine job.

The floor lead gets help, not replacement.

Quality checking, yes, and better, because every call gets checked instead of three out
of a hundred.

The compliance desk, yes. That should never have been a spreadsheet.

The back office clerk chasing yesterday's promises, yes.

And the last row. Field, legal, and hardship. Somebody who lost their job. A death in
the family. We do not touch that. A machine has no business in that conversation.

So we drew the line there, and we left it there.

SLIDE 8 - ONE SYSTEM, FOUR LAYERS
=====================================================================

Four layers.

The mouth. Voice, phone line, WhatsApp, SMS. It switches between Hindi and English in
the middle of a call.

The brains. A set of engines that decide what is allowed. Not the AI model. Plain code.

The hands. Twenty four things the agent is allowed to do. Check identity, record a
promise, raise a dispute, send documents, transfer to a human. It cannot do anything
outside that list.

And memory. Around a hundred and fifty tables. Customers, loans, promises, disputes,
consent, and a log of everything that ever happened.

If a better voice model comes out next month, the top layer gets swapped in a day. The
three layers under it are the real product.


=====================================================================
SLIDE 9 - THE MODEL NEVER DECIDES
=====================================================================

This is the one rule everything follows.

The model never decides. It only speaks.

The AI does not choose the waiver. It does not choose whether to call. It does not
decide who gets a settlement.

The engines run first. They pull the facts. They apply the blocks, like do not disturb,
calling hours, and how many times we already called. Then they score what is left, pick
one, and write it all down.

Only then does the model get told, say this. Its only job is to say it well, in the
right language.

It looks like less AI. It is less guessing. Anything that skips this looks brilliant in
a demo and turns into a legal problem in production.


=====================================================================
SLIDE 10 - FOUR BRAINS
=====================================================================

Four engines sit behind that one voice.

Reco decides what can be offered, with caps, so the same customer does not get spammed.

Treatment decides what to do next. Stay quiet, send an SMS, WhatsApp, call, send a
human, or go legal. Everything is scored in rupees. Doing nothing scores zero, so any
action has to be worth more than doing nothing.

Authority is the money one. On a live call it says, this much can be approved, this much
cannot, and this needs a supervisor. A human telecaller is not allowed to hand out
waivers on his own. So instead of hoping the AI behaves, that policy is written into
code.

And live QA. Every turn of every call is scored while it is happening. If something
serious goes wrong, it can cut into the call live.


=====================================================================
SLIDE 11 - A MESH, NOT A MONOLOGUE
=====================================================================

It is not one bot doing everything. It is a small team of them.

Intake checks who you are, then hands over to collections. From there, hardship goes to
a specialist and then to a human. Dispute goes one way. Insurance goes another. And if
the amount is above what it can approve, it goes to a supervisor.

The handover is a proper action with a rule behind it. The AI cannot simply say the
words transferring you to legal and have it happen. That is far too easy to trick.

Bottom left are the hard rails. Sixty one days late, the bot does not stay on the call.
Do not disturb, no voice at all. Customer sounds upset, no selling. Outside eight to
seven, the call never even dials.

SLIDE 13 - THREE LAYERS YOU CAN EDIT
=====================================================================

Three layers you can edit, and one you cannot.

IF SOMEBODY ASKS
=====================================================================

Why not just write a better prompt?
Because a prompt cannot be audited. When RBI asks who approved that waiver, the answer
has to be a row in a table with a name and a timestamp, not a sentence in a transcript.

How fast is it really?
Warm calls answer in around half a second to three quarters. First response from the
model is one and a half to two seconds. Keeping the connections warm was the fix.

Are those McKinsey numbers yours?
No. There has been no pilot. Those are published industry figures, used to size the
opportunity, nothing more.

Why does the AI do so little?
It does the one thing it is genuinely good at, which is talking to a person in their own
language. Everything involving money or the law is code, because code can be tested and
defended.

What is left to build?
The studio, so a business user can create agents. The bank system connectors. And
turning the shadow engines on for real.

What is a canary?
You do not put a new voice on every customer at once. Ten percent get the new
version, the rest stay on the old one. Same customer always gets the same version.
If calls get slow, live QA starts failing, or an eval breaks, it rolls back by
itself. A split with no rollback is not allowed to publish. A hundred percent is
just a full ship.

How do evals work?
They are tests, not another AI judging the transcript. We write situations, like
promise without identity, or the bot saying transfer to legal in words. Code
checks whether the CRM row actually appeared. Regression is still-works. Red team
is somebody trying to trick it. If those fail, it does not go live. Skipped means
we have not run it, not a fake pass.
